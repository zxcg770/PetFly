# Pet Import Regulations — Germany

## Overview
Germany follows EU pet travel rules. Dogs, cats, and ferrets entering Germany must have a valid EU pet passport, a microchip, and an up-to-date rabies vaccination. Dogs may also require tapeworm treatment depending on their country of origin. No quarantine is required for compliant pets.

## Required Documents
- EU pet passport (mandatory for dogs, cats, and ferrets)
- Microchip certificate (recorded in the passport)
- Rabies vaccination record (recorded in the passport)
- Tapeworm treatment record (dogs only, depending on origin country)

## Microchip Requirements
- Standard: ISO 11784/11785
- The microchip must be implanted before or at the same time as the first rabies vaccination
- If the microchip is implanted after the first rabies vaccination, the vaccination must be repeated after the microchip is in place

## EU Pet Passport
- All dogs, cats, and ferrets entering Germany must have a valid EU pet passport
- The passport must be issued by an authorised veterinarian in an EU member state
- It must contain: microchip number, owner details, vaccination records, and treatment records
- Pets from non-EU countries require a third-country equivalent document that meets EU standards

## Vaccination Requirements
- Rabies vaccination is mandatory
- The primary vaccination is only valid after a 21-day waiting period from the date of administration
- Booster vaccinations must be given before the previous vaccination expires
- Typical validity: 1 year or 3 years depending on the vaccine product used
- The vaccination date and expiry must be recorded in the EU pet passport

## Tapeworm Treatment (Dogs Only)
- Required for dogs entering Germany from certain non-EU countries and some EU countries with specific risk profiles
- Must be administered between 1 and 5 days before arrival in Germany
- The active ingredient must be praziquantel (minimum 50mg) or equivalent
- The treatment date, product name, and veterinarian's signature must be recorded in the pet passport
- Dogs travelling only within the EU (e.g. from France to Germany) are generally exempt, but check the specific origin country

## Quarantine Rules
- No quarantine is required for pets entering with a valid EU pet passport and complete documentation
- Pets from high-risk rabies countries may be subject to additional checks at the border

## Age Restrictions
- Puppies and kittens must be at least 15 weeks old to enter Germany
- This is calculated as: 12 weeks minimum age for primary vaccination + 21-day waiting period
- Younger animals cannot be legally imported, even with documentation

## Non-EU Pets
- Pets from non-EU countries must meet additional requirements
- A rabies antibody titer test may be required depending on the origin country's risk classification
- The titer test must show a result of at least 0.5 IU/ml
- A 3-month waiting period after a successful titer test may apply for high-risk countries

## Airline Policies
Airline pet policies vary widely and are separate from government import regulations — both must be satisfied.
- **Lufthansa**: Allows small dogs and cats in the cabin (in an IATA-approved carrier under the seat) on most flights to/from Germany, subject to a combined pet + carrier weight limit (typically around 8 kg). Larger pets travel as checked baggage.
- **Air China**: Does not allow pets in the cabin on flights between Germany and China. Pets must travel as checked baggage or air cargo.
- **Turkish Airlines**: Allows small pets in the cabin on flights between Germany and Turkey, subject to carrier size and weight limits.
- Always confirm the current cabin/cargo policy, carrier size limits, and any advance booking requirements directly with the airline — policies can change and may differ by route.

## Important Warnings
- The 21-day waiting period after primary rabies vaccination is mandatory — a pet vaccinated the day before travel is not compliant
- Always check whether your origin country requires tapeworm treatment before entry into Germany
- Puppies born during relocation must be at least 15 weeks old before they can travel
