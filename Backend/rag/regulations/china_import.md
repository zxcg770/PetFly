# Pet Import Regulations — China

## Overview
China has strict pet import requirements. All dogs and cats entering China must meet microchip, vaccination, titer test, and health certificate requirements. Quarantine on arrival is mandatory.

## Required Documents
- Microchip certificate
- Rabies vaccination record
- Rabies titer test result (from approved laboratory)
- Official health certificate endorsed by national authority
- Import permit (in some cases — check with destination city authority)

## Microchip Requirements
- Standard: ISO 11784/11785
- The microchip must be implanted before any vaccinations are administered
- If the microchip is implanted after the first rabies vaccination, the vaccination series must be restarted

## Vaccination Requirements
- Rabies vaccination is mandatory for all dogs and cats
- The vaccination must be administered after microchip implantation
- First-time (primary) vaccinations must be given at least 30 days before the date of travel
- The vaccination must be valid — not expired — at time of entry
- Typical validity: 1 year for annual vaccines, 3 years for triennial vaccines (check your specific product)

## Rabies Titer Test
- A rabies antibody titer test is required for all dogs and cats entering China
- Blood sample must be drawn at least 30 days after the rabies vaccination
- The result must show an antibody level of at least 0.5 IU/ml
- The test must be performed at a laboratory approved by the Chinese authorities
- The titer test result must be presented alongside the health certificate

## Health Certificate
- Must be issued by an accredited (official) veterinarian
- Must be issued within 10 days of the travel date
- Must be endorsed/stamped by the national competent authority (e.g. USDA APHIS in the US, APHA in the UK, BVET in Germany)
- The certificate must list: microchip number, vaccination dates, titer test result, owner details, and destination

## Quarantine Rules
- All dogs and cats entering China are subject to mandatory quarantine inspection at the port of entry
- Standard quarantine period: 7 days at a government-designated quarantine facility
- The quarantine period can be extended up to 30 days if documentation is incomplete or the animal shows signs of illness
- The owner is responsible for all quarantine costs (facility fees, veterinary inspections)
- Owners cannot stay with their pet during quarantine

## Approved Entry Ports
- Not all international airports in China accept imported pets
- Approved entry ports include: Beijing Capital International Airport, Shanghai Pudong International Airport, Guangzhou Baiyun International Airport
- Always confirm with your airline and the Chinese customs authority before booking

## Breed Restrictions
- Certain dog breeds are subject to restrictions or bans in major Chinese cities
- Beijing bans dogs with a shoulder height above 35cm in urban districts
- Check the specific regulations for your destination city before travel

## Airline Policies
Airline pet policies vary widely and are separate from government import regulations — both must be satisfied.
- **Air China**: Does not allow pets in the cabin on international flights. Pets must travel as checked baggage or air cargo, in an IATA-approved travel crate.
- **Lufthansa**: Allows small dogs and cats in the cabin (in an IATA-approved carrier under the seat) on flights to China, subject to a combined pet + carrier weight limit (typically around 8 kg). Larger pets travel as checked baggage.
- **China Eastern / China Southern**: Generally do not allow pets in the cabin on international routes; pets travel as checked baggage or cargo, similar to Air China.
- Always confirm the current cabin/cargo policy, carrier size limits, and any advance booking requirements directly with the airline — policies can change and may differ by route.

## Important Warnings
- Start the process at least 3–4 months before travel — the titer test alone requires 30 days after vaccination, plus lab processing time
- Health certificates expire quickly (10-day window) — book your vet appointment as close to departure as possible
- Always verify current requirements with the Chinese embassy or consulate in your country, as regulations can change
